Mechanism of Action

Liposem's balanced dual-agonism addresses two critical metabolic pathways simultaneously — delivering outcomes neither approach achieves alone.

PPAR-α Activation (Predominant)
🫀

Lipid & Hepatic Axis

Predominantly activates PPAR-α receptors in the liver, driving lipid catabolism, reducing VLDL production, and enhancing fatty acid oxidation.

  • Upregulates lipoprotein lipase (LPL) — accelerated TG clearance
  • Reduces VLDL synthesis in hepatocytes
  • Enhances fatty acid β-oxidation
  • Reversal of hepatic steatosis (MASLD/NASH)
⚡ Target: Triglycerides ↓45–60% · ALT/AST normalization · LSM & CAP score reduction
PPAR-γ Activation (Moderate)
🔬

Insulin & Glucose Axis

Moderately activates PPAR-γ in muscle and adipose tissue — improving insulin sensitivity without the extreme edema or weight gain of classical thiazolidinediones.

  • Increases GLUT4 expression — enhanced glucose uptake
  • Improves peripheral insulin sensitivity
  • Reduces fasting insulin and HOMA-IR
  • Lowers HbA1c by 0.7%–1.5% absolute
⚡ Target: HbA1c ↓0.7–1.5% · Fasting glucose ↓ · HOMA-IR improvement

Clinical Indications

Built for the Most Challenging Metabolic Profiles

Precision-targeted therapy for patient populations where conventional agents fall short.

01

Diabetic Dyslipidemia

For T2DM patients with elevated triglycerides uncontrolled by statin monotherapy — where the residual metabolic risk demands a dual-action approach.

  • TG >150 mg/dL despite statin therapy
  • Mixed hyperlipidemia in T2DM
  • High-risk cardiovascular phenotype
  • Non-alcoholic fatty liver with T2DM
02

MASLD / NASH

Directly targets hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis — reducing FibroScan-measured liver stiffness (LSM) and Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP) scores.

  • Elevated ALT/AST with fatty liver
  • Biopsy-confirmed NASH
  • LSM >7 kPa on transient elastography
  • MASLD with concurrent T2DM/dyslipidemia
03

Hypertriglyceridemia

Primary hypertriglyceridemia unresponsive to lifestyle modification and conventional fibrate therapy — particularly in South Asian high-TG phenotypes.

  • Severe hypertriglyceridemia (>500 mg/dL)
  • Fibrate-resistant triglyceride elevation
  • Post-prandial lipemia
  • Familial combined hyperlipidemia

Co-Administration with Statins (Real World Study)

Co-administration with ongoing statin therapy offers additional benefits, with no adverse interactions observed. Saroglitazar in combination with statins exhibited consistently greater reductions. At 96 weeks, LSM declined by 51.5% in the combination group versus 30.4% with Saroglitazar alone (p<0.001), with this differential sustained at 144 weeks (49.9% vs 46.5%). Similarly, CAP values decreased more substantially in the statin group across all timepoints, with a maximal reduction of 22.6% at 96 weeks.

Quick Reference

Frequently Asked Questions

Liposem (Saroglitazar Magnesium) is dosed at 4 mg once daily, taken orally with or without food. The 2 mg tablet form is also available for titration. The drug reaches steady-state plasma concentrations within 3–5 days. No dose adjustment is required in patients with mild-to-moderate renal impairment, given its predominantly hepatic clearance.
Saroglitazar is a unique dual PPAR α/γ agonist — not a pure fibrate (PPAR-α only) or a thiazolidinedione (PPAR-γ only). Its predominant PPAR-α activity delivers superior triglyceride reduction compared to fibrates, while its moderate PPAR-γ activity improves glycemic control without the severe weight gain, edema, or fracture risk associated with full PPAR-γ agonists like Pioglitazone.
Yes. Saroglitazar undergoes predominantly hepatic clearance via glucuronidation and CYP2C8-mediated oxidation, with minimal renal elimination. Clinical data supports use in CKD stages 1–3 without dose adjustment. Caution is advised in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). Regular liver function monitoring is recommended as with all hepatically-cleared drugs.
Triglyceride reduction is typically observed within 4–8 weeks of initiation. HbA1c reduction follows a glycemic timeline, with meaningful reductions at 12–16 weeks. Hepatic benefits (ALT normalization, LSM reduction) in MASLD/NASH patients are typically evident at 24 weeks of continuous therapy, with progressive improvement on sustained treatment.
